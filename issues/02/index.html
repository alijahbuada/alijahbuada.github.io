A Vocabulary of What Makes a Film Perfect

by Alijah Jacob Buada

These are not borrowed terms. I invented these tropes after watching the following films and trying to name what kept happening, the specific things that made them feel true rather than constructed, felt rather than made. For sake of ease when I use the term film, I am using it as a blanket term for all listed media. I include anime, manga, limited series, and films.

A “perfect” film just keeps being right, in ways you cannot fully explain until you try.

It would seem, looking at the films listed here, that most of them belong to the last decade. This is not ignorance of film history. It is not recency bias. It is something closer to the opposite: a willingness to believe that modern film has more accurately captured the human experience now than at any other point in the medium's history, precisely because it has had longer to try.

We are working from a vast and documented database of human experience within film itself. Every film made today is made in conversation with every film that came before it. The medium has simply had more time to get it right. These films feel true to me not because they are new, but because they are the most recent beneficiaries of everything that came before them.

reel 1

dialogue and inference

対話
no. 01dialogue and inference

Cross-Pollination of Mumbled Nothingness

(Cross-Pollination) n.

…so anyway, Dialogue that arrives mid-thought. Characters speak as though the conversation began before the scene did, ideas accumulating across registers, articulate in feeling if not always in structure. The film trusts the viewer to feel the through-line before they see it. Somewhat articulately well spoken and earnest.

no. 02dialogue and inference

Forced Inference

(Assumed Understanding) n.

The film withholds the connective tissue between scenes, emotions, and revelations, assuming the viewer will supply it. When they do, the feeling is specific: being trusted. The scratching of the ego in the right place. The reward for staying present.

no. 03dialogue and inference

Jazz as Silence Killer

(Not a Lead Enforcer) n.

Music that appears between moments rather than within them. It fills transitions, not scenes. It does not lead the emotion. It kills the silence after the emotion has already happened. The score knows its place.

reel 2

character interiority and code

内面
no. 04character interiority and code

Anti-Nostalgia

(The Past is Someone Else) n.

A relationship to the past that refuses sentimentality. The character's history is acknowledged as fact and then released. Not repression, active non-attachment. The past is real. It belongs to someone else now. I am just me.

no. 05character interiority and code

Master Code Switcher

(Borderline MPD) n.

A protagonist who speaks an entirely different language with each person they encounter, not performing, adapting. The register shifts completely. Every version is real. The character is all of them simultaneously and none of them permanently.

no. 06character interiority and code

Alien-Like / Alien Speak

(The Sore Thumb) n.

A character who communicates from a frequency others cannot quite receive, not because they are strange but because they are accurate. They say true things in the wrong register, at the wrong moment, with the wrong affect. Both the alien-like and the alien speak make you stick out like a sore thumb.

no. 07character interiority and code

Beautiful Ugly

(A Compromised Color Scheme) n.

A cast of characters whose specific wrongness produces something unexpectedly beautiful in aggregate. None of them are right. All of them together are. Each person represents a different shade of broken, and the film holds them all in the same frame without resolving any of them into something tidier. The ugliness is the palette. The beauty is what that palette produces.

reel 3

the body and gesture

身体
no. 08the body and gesture

Unorthodox Gesticulation

(Natural Asymmetrical Movement) n.

Physical performance that was not choreographed, or was choreographed to look like it was not. Natural, asymmetrical, slightly wrong by conventional acting standards and exactly right by human ones. The body knows things the script does not say.

no. 09the body and gesture

Sexondary

(Sex as Secondary) n.

Sexuality present in the atmosphere of a film without being its engine. The erotic is incidental, a fact of the world rather than the point of the scene. The past is stated, not performed. The body is context, not content.

no. 10the body and gesture

Wispy Bangs Make for Wispy Thoughts

(Styling as Psychology) n.

The hair that falls into the eyes. The softness of appearance against the precision of gaze. In a perfect film, costume and styling are character, not symbolism applied from outside but psychology made visible. The way someone wears their face tells you who they are before they speak.

reel 4

visual language

映像
no. 11visual language

Random Item Fixation

(Empty Symbolism of the Close-Up Cutaway) n.

An object receives close-up attention it has not narratively earned. The camera holds on it longer than necessary. The symbolism is withheld. The viewer is invited to supply meaning, or to sit in the emptiness of a thing that just exists. The absence of explanation is the explanation.

no. 12visual language

Breathless Transitions

(Former Latter, No Matter) n.

Scene changes that do not announce themselves. Time passes without notice. The film moves through seasons the way a person moves through years, not marked by event but by the quiet accumulation of presence. Former, latter, no matter.

no. 13visual language

The Held Shot

(Weather on a Face) n.

The camera stays on a face longer than comfort allows. Not a close-up for information but a close-up for weather. You watch someone experience something in real time. Incompatible emotions held simultaneously, not edited around. Witnessed. The refusal to cut is the statement.

no. 14visual language

Toppling Inconveniences

(Consequently Causing Concern) n.

Structural looseness that looks like weakness until you realize it is how the film breathes. Not every seam is a flaw. Some films hold together through feeling rather than architecture,

( exhale )

and when you notice a gap, it is usually the film exhaling. The concern for how it is all put together is part of the experience.

reel 5

the seeing problem

視覚
no. 15the seeing problem

Mature Thoughtful Children Juxtaposing Cold Adults

(Child-Adult Inversion) n.

The children are porous. The adults are sealed. A child in a perfect film understands something the adult has forgotten or refused to know. The child's emotional honesty makes the adult's composure look like damage.

no. 16the seeing problem

Someone Blind Who Cannot See / Someone Who Sees But Is Blinded

(You Can See Right Through Me) n.

Two characters who together form one complete act of seeing. One can observe clearly from the outside but lacks the interiority to understand. One has total interiority but cannot locate themselves. Together they complete each other's vision without either knowing it.

reel 6

ensemble and relationship

群像
no. 17ensemble and relationship

The Parentless Make Great Parents

(Self-Explanatory) n.

Characters who were never parented, or parented badly, who become the most instinctive, generous caregivers in the room. Not in spite of their absence of parents but because of it. They know what someone needed and never got because they needed it too.

no. 18ensemble and relationship

Drifters Find Each Other

(Bustling Shells in a Soft Current) n.

Characters who are not looking for community and find it anyway, not through action or intention but through proximity and time. They wash up next to each other. That is enough. Like bustling shells in a soft current.

no. 19ensemble and relationship

Band of Empties Finding Solace

(Until They Cannot, or Have To Stop) n.

An ensemble of characters who are hollow in their own specific ways, different shapes of empty, who find in each other a temporary fullness. Not permanent. Not promised. Just enough, for now, until the current changes.

no. 20ensemble and relationship

Confidante That Knows Their Place

(But Knows the MC Ought Not To) n.

A secondary character who understands the protagonist's arc more clearly than the protagonist does, and knows their role is to witness, not to intervene. They know where the story is going. They know the main character is not supposed to stay.

reel 7

not a checklist

採点

These tropes are not a checklist. But try it anyway: pick a film you love, tap the terms it has, and see where it lands. Call Me Chihiro is loaded first, as the anchor.
