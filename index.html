debut novel · 2026

Memory is fragile. So is he.

In a quiet town, an unnamed man drifts through the soft routine of his life. Then a stranger calls his phone, a little girl appears at his door, and his dreams start to sharpen until dreaming and living can't be told apart.

from chapter 1 · morning in kanagawa

The willow trees danced as the wind hurried by. The new season had begun rounding its corner, and I knew that much.

Outside, faint traces of sea salt hovered in the morning draft—the kind that sneaks inland from Sagami Bay before the sun decides whether it wants to appear. The power lines swayed like skipping ropes. Far in the distance, a train announced itself, rolling through the valley between rows of apartment blocks. Kanagawa always wakes in fragments: wind first, then concrete, then people.

Somewhere, a cat was cleaning itself.

about

自己紹介the long version

I grew up in San Diego. Spanish was my first language at home, and English came with my siblings once school started. I've been switching between registers ever since.

I studied Television, Film, and New Media at San Diego State, with an emphasis in critical studies, and graduated summa cum laude.

For four years I worked at Your Legacy Your Story, a digitizing and storytelling company that turns family archives into documentaries and books. I started in the lab and ended up running production, which mostly meant taking someone's old tapes, photos, and voice and shaping them into something their family could keep.

Now I'm stepping into healthcare as a medical assistant, with a focus on pediatrics. I still make films, write fiction, and compose on the side. I watch a lot, read a lot, and treat taking things in and making things as the same act.
